About

Language variation meets speech representation learning.

I am a PhD student in the Department of Computational Linguistics at the University of Zurich. My research brings together automatic speech recognition, speech representation learning, and experimental phonetics to understand how speech models handle regional variation.

I am particularly interested in whether models preserve lexical tone, how phonological knowledge can improve recognition across Mandarin dialects, and how acoustic evidence interacts with semantic context.

  • Mandarin dialects
  • Automatic speech recognition
  • Lexical tone
  • Speech representation learning

Current research

Ongoing 2025–

Phonology-informed ASR Adaptation across Mandarin Dialects

This project examines whether explicit tone and phone information can improve pretrained ASR models across 14 Mandarin dialects. It compares multiple model architectures and studies where phonology-informed adaptation changes recognition errors.

With Eleanor Chodroff

Ongoing 2026–

Lexical Tone and Semantic Context in Mandarin ASR

We are developing a controlled speech corpus of tone-based minimal pairs in Standard and Chengdu Mandarin. The project tests how ASR systems balance acoustic tone cues with semantic context when the two sources of information point toward different words.

With Eleanor Chodroff

Human-like cross-language generalisation in deep neural speaker embeddings and its acoustic foundations

Tianze Xu*, Xiyang Li*, Xiaoming Jiang, and Volker Dellwo

* Equal contribution

We compare voice-similarity judgments from human listeners with representations from 18 speaker-embedding models using Cantonese–English bilingual speech, examining whether model generalisation mirrors human perception and acoustic structure.
